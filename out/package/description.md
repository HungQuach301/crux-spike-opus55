A $25,000 car loan at 5.2% with 48 months left: send the extra cash to the loan (road A), or invest it (road B)? We compute where the answer flips. This is a threshold, not a forecast, and not advice.

Result
- Road A pays the loan off at month 28 and avoids $1,190 of interest. That part is certain.
- Break-even average annual return (compounded), after tax at month 48: 5.23% (12% bracket), 6.11% (22%), 6.26% (32%, including the 3.8% NIIT).
- One ILLUSTRATIVE sequence (8% a year for 36 months, then −20%): road A ends $459 ahead.

Assumptions (all stated, none forecast)
- Loan: $25,000 balance, 5.2% APR, 48 months left, payment $578 (standard amortization).
- Extra cash: $400 a month, available to either road. ILLUSTRATIVE.
- Returns: a constant average annual return (compounded monthly as (1+r)^(1/12)); the 2%–10% range is ILLUSTRATIVE.
- Tax: each monthly contribution is its own lot, sold at month 48. Lots held more than 12 months are long-term. Rates (ordinary / long-term): 12% / 0%, 22% / 15%, 32% bracket 35.8% / 18.8% with NIIT.
- Losing lots get no deduction. Loan interest is not deductible; the model ignores the 2025–2028 new-vehicle loan interest deduction. State income tax is ignored. US only.
